# RE86 full-standard characterization and generator audit

## Result

RE86 has eight shipped levels, so the full curriculum is exactly
`DIFFICULTIES == (1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8)` with native contexts `0..7`.
The family implementation now exposes both `generate(seed, difficulty, split=...)`
and `generate_game(seed, split=...)`; the latter returns eight increasing tiers
and can be replayed as one native episode without forced transitions.

The implementation covers every mechanic used by the shipped levels, produces
new procedural layouts and target assignments, and validates constructive
positive certificates in the intended native context. It does not claim that
constructive or teacher routes are shortest. After independent review closed
the three substantive blockers and the focused direct-API admission regression
passed, root authorized `FULL_STANDARD_CONTRACT.status == "ready"` for final
copy and post-copy verification.

## Authoritative source and engine inventory

The characterized source is `third_party/arc3_games/re86.py`, SHA-256
`cf3c1520e17f0b70e7a3cea0e680601a591befce306b4dd1926c735a7d3b65e8`.
The relevant source regions are:

- official level declarations: lines 1615–1753;
- environment setup and selected-shape state: lines 1758–1893;
- win test, cyclic selection, and selected-centre normalization: lines
  1894–1941;
- three-pixel translation, bounds, obstacle collision, ordinary deformation,
  and flexible resizing: lines 1943–2093;
- dye contact, progressive flood-fill frames, and secondary colour state:
  lines 2076–2122;
- action dispatch, native step budget, and level completion: lines 2124–2158.

The five public actions are four cardinal translations and cyclic selection.
They have no meaningful click coordinates. Each translation is three pixels.
The shipped mechanic inventory is:

1. selection among multiple independently movable sprites;
2. ordinary rigid translation and selected-centre normalization;
3. flexible sprites whose dimensions change on obstacle contact;
4. fixed-centre sprites whose centre remains coloured when unselected;
5. ordinary rigid pixel deformation or a blocked move on obstacle contact;
6. dyes that recolour the contacted pixel and then animate a progressive
   flood fill across the connected sprite;
7. same-colour/ambiguous target ownership and overlapping target coverage;
8. target-constrained selection centres, where selection state itself changes
   target agreement;
9. per-level native budgets and sequential level-index/context progression.

There are no shipped click or undo mechanics to fabricate. Tests reject click
coordinates and unsupported action IDs and use reset as the applicable live
recovery operation.

## Teacher design and proof boundary

The bounded teacher models mutable sprite pixels, positions, sizes, fixed and
flexible centre rules, static obstacles, rigid deformation, flexible resizing,
dye colour/flood-fill stable states, target masks, and current selection.
RE86 movables do not collide with each other, so the teacher enumerates each
movable's exact state independently, joins candidate target signatures, adds
selection actions, and then replays the combined route through the real engine.

Randomized transition parity checks against the vendored engine covered all
eight official levels during implementation. The focused regression then
found and replayed a positive teacher route for every official tier and replayed
all eight sequentially in one episode. `SearchResult` distinguishes positive,
truncated, unsupported, and exhausted results. Positive routes are exact with
respect to the modeled transition and native replay, but `optimal=False` is
deliberate: the factored join seeks a bounded witness, not a globally shortest
route.

`solution_mechanics()` derives facts from a fresh replay rather than trusting
tags or stored counters. It records selection, distinct selected sprites,
movement, dye/flood animation, resize, ordinary deformation, blocked movement,
fixed/flexible selections, target-constrained centres, same-colour ambiguity,
action count, and native win.

## Procedural generation and admission

Generator version 4 uses fresh combinations of upstream sprite prototypes,
positions, routes, dye placements, obstacle interactions, and target masks.
The official layouts and routes are never generator inputs. A target is
derived from the stable result of a constructive native route, after which its
anchors and complete 3x3 guide cues are sampled from owned sprite pixels.

The tier grammar introduces the official progression:

- tiers 1–3: multi-sprite translation, then flexible pieces and ambiguous
  same-colour assignment;
- tiers 4–5: dyes/flood animation and fixed centres, then a mixed
  rigid/fixed/flexible composition;
- tier 6: obstacle-driven flexible resize;
- tier 7: dyes, flexible resize, and ordinary rigid deformation in one route;
- tier 8: two flexible pieces, two obstacles, fourteen dyes, and repeated
  resize interactions.

Tier 1 and tier 8 use eight seed-derived target-distance route families. These
are real endpoint/required-work changes within the reference action bands, not
post-win padding or proof labels. Other tiers retain bounded finite procedural
grammars; cosmetic palette or coordinate jitter is not counted as semantic
route diversity.

Accepted rows carry versioned source, generator, difficulty, mechanics,
quality, geometry, public-gameplay, and private-route identities. Geometry and
public gameplay are canonicalized over all D4 rotations/reflections on the
fixed 64x64 board; they do not erase boundary-sensitive translation. Ordered
sprite lists remain ordered. Geometry normalizes cosmetic palette while
retaining shape, special pixels, roles, order, dimensions, and coordinates.
Public gameplay additionally retains transition-relevant palette, targets,
dye render/colour, context, and budget. It excludes certificates, solutions,
seeds, and split labels. `solution_semantic_sha256` alone canonicalizes the
action sequence and route events. Public gameplay hash modulo three is the
train/validation/test partition. Official ordered canonical geometry hashes
are rejected by generation and recomputed by validation.

`validate_full_standard()` rebuilds and recomputes:

- source/game/format/version and exact tier/context fields;
- structural metrics and official-derived profile gates;
- geometry identity, split partition, and gameplay identity;
- full native-context route replay, first terminal win on the final action,
  native budget, and all solution-mechanic counters;
- proof mirrors and tamper-sensitive action/identity fields;
- 64x64 integer native frames, complete/readable target cues, and unclipped
  intermediate witness presentation.

No proof boolean, stored counter, or declared hash is accepted on trust.

## Limitations and review status

- Calibration has one official level per tier. Tolerances are deliberate
  engineering bounds, not evidence of the unseen population distribution.
- Positive teacher and generated certificates do not establish global
  shortest paths. Search cutoffs leave solvability unknown.
- The procedural grammar is finite. In the final sample tier 4 produced seven
  distinct normalized action routes across eight distinct geometries.
- Public partitioning uses the D4-normalized gameplay identity. Ordered,
  color-blind geometry and private D4-normalized route diversity are separate
  diagnostics; route diversity is not a proof of active-topology diversity or
  learned generalization, and fixed-board translations remain significant.
- Review found a longer 60-action tier-7 win with dye and resize but no rigid
  deformation, versus the 48-action constructive and 41-action deletion-shortened
  witnesses that do deform. This is a mechanic-optional longer route caveat,
  not evidence of an easier bypass or a reason to require every installed
  device on every possible win.
- Presentation checks are programmatic native-frame checks; no desktop or
  browser UI was opened, as required by the task constraints.
- No learned-policy training, GPU work, held-out `m0r0`, or full-repository
  pytest was run.
- Teacher/planner semantics are unchanged. At the deliberately tiny
  `node_limit=1`, accounting can report three per-movable expansions before
  truncation; joined-work caps and ordinary tier budgets retain their prior
  semantics.
- Contract status is `ready` under root's conditional authorization; root owns
  final copy and post-copy verification.
