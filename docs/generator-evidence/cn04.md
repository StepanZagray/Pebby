## Native mechanics and official characterization

Source evidence is in `third_party/arc3_games/cn04.py:699-1182`.

- CN04 groups sprites by coincident initial top-left coordinates in coordinate
  insertion order. It stable-sorts alternatives by layer, so equal-layer ties
  retain original level order. Authored `group` and `alternate` labels have no
  native effect.
- One visible member exists per group. The initially visible sprite nearest the
  origin is selected. Selection changes body presentation; tiers 3-6 grey all
  unselected sprites and reveal the selected sprite's original colours.
- Pins use pristine rendered pixels. A pin is satisfied only when exactly two
  pins of the same intrinsic colour occupy one coordinate. Mixed-colour pairs,
  triples, and unmatched pins do not complete the level.
- ACTION1-4 moves the selected piece one cell within its rendered bounding box.
  Pieces may overlap. Even a blocked move checks completion.
- ACTION5 rotates a singleton, but cycles a stack. It clamps the newly visible
  member and checks completion. Clicking a selected zero-valued stack cell also
  cycles, without clamping and without checking completion.
- All stacks share one forward/backward direction bit. Cycling bounces at an
  endpoint and transfers the old visible member's position to the new member;
  each member keeps its fixed rotation.
- Click resolution uses visible level order and rendered opaque pixels.
  Completion advances after a winning ACTION1-5 transition. ACTION6 cannot
  finish a level.
- Only `MaxSteps - 1` actions are usable because the engine checks the loss
  boundary before applying the next action. There is no undo or collision rule.

The exact symbolic state is `(group transforms, active alternates, selected
group, global cycle direction)`. It models both cycling controls, clamp timing,
click precedence, exact colour/multiplicity matching, completion timing, and
the remaining native budget.

## Generated grammar

Every tier samples fresh connected asymmetric bodies, initial rotations,
non-overlapping visible starts, backgrounds, relation coordinates, edge
colours, and connected relation multigraphs. Each relation is one exact
same-colour two-pin match at a distinct coordinate. The generator permits up to
three parallel edges because tier 5 requires exactly three between its 7-pin
stack winner and the 3-pin singleton.

The active winning degrees exactly match the table above. Tier 5 contains the
exact `(3, 4, 5, 6, 7)` stack spectrum and selects the 7-pin member. Tier 6
contains the exact `(2, 3, 3, 4, 4, 6)` and `(3, 3, 3, 3)` spectra. The second
tier-6 stack deliberately remains ambiguous: every alternate has three pins,
and equal-count decoys preserve the winner's per-colour pin multiset. Repeated
three- and four-pin decoys in the first stack also share their count/colour
spectra. Tier 4 preserves the official one-pin-colour rule.

Tiers 5 and 6 require ACTION5 stack use in accepted witnesses, and tier 6
requires both stack roles. Zero-cell click cycling and endpoint reversal are
covered by independent native regressions because neither is needed in the
accepted direct winning routes. No no-op, out-and-back, or remote-final-target
padding is added.

Generation constructs a private feasible target only to reject malformed
drafts. The stored certificate comes from a public-state teacher that does not
read the target, route, hashes, proof flags, or winning annotations.

## Teacher and bounded-search semantics

The generated teacher enumerates public alternate assignments, connected exact
pin assemblies, minimum-total-translation placements, and legal piece orders.
It never filters candidates against the quality action band. Changing
`PROFILES[difficulty]["witness_actions"]` leaves its route and work unchanged;
the generator separately rejects a genuinely easy or overly long candidate.

Public tier composition and pin multiplicity prune stack choices. This is
source-faithful: tier 5's 7-pin winner and tier 6's first 2-pin winner are
unique in their official spectra, while tier 6's all-three-pin stack retains
four candidate choices. Returned routes are independently replayed on a native
clone and are positive witnesses only; no optimality is claimed.

Generated assembly work has a 100,000-operation family cap and an 8,000-work
cap per alternate assignment. A successful route records total work, its exact
teacher reason, and the number of earlier capped assignments. Validation reruns
the teacher and compares all three. If a skipped assignment prevents a positive
witness, the result is `truncated`; it is never called exhaustive or unsolvable.
Official layouts continue through the bounded exact-state planner.

## Identities, split, and proof validation

`native_semantic_groups` reconstructs native coordinate groups in insertion
order and alternatives by stable `(layer, original order)`. Consistent arbitrary
renaming of ignored `group` and `alternate` labels leaves raw geometry, D4
geometry, gameplay identity, partition, native frames, and completion unchanged.
Contradictory labels fail closed.

The SHA-256 geometry identities contain actual initial cells, canonical native
group/alternate positions, initial transforms, and pin/body kinds. D4 geometry
is canonical over translation and all eight square symmetries, then determines
the train/validation/test partition. Gameplay identity binds actual initial
native semantics, including order, layer, visibility, budget, and masking, but
excludes seed, labels, private targets, and certificates. Exact normalized
official sprite-set copies are rejected.

`validate_full_standard` recomputes structure, identities, split, official-copy
evidence, strict action triples, terminal relation constraints, mechanic trace,
budget, native transition parity, first completion on the final action, and the
native contextual win. It also reruns the bounded teacher to verify the route,
work, reason, and assignment-cap count. Boolean-as-integer and malformed proof
mutations are rejected. `build_game` validates exactly six ordered, distinct,
same-split rows and replays their stored witnesses in one uninterrupted native
episode.

Primitive admission now runs before NumPy or native-engine coercion. Every
piece must provide exact integer grouping, position, layer, and canonical
quarter-turn rotation fields, an exact boolean visibility field, and a nonempty
rectangular pixel array whose entries are exact integers in the native `-2..15`
domain. That domain retains both native transparency sentinels (`-1` and `-2`)
and all 16 display colours. Version and curriculum values use type-sensitive
comparison, required proof keys remain required even when their expected value
is `None`, and recomputed mechanics/constraint trees are compared with exact
primitive types.

## Retained limits

1. Each tier has one official reference. Tolerances are explicit engineering
   bounds rather than confidence intervals.
2. Generated and official teachers return replayed positive witnesses. Minimum
   translation is conditional on the selected assembly, not a globally shortest
   solution. Capped negative searches and assignment truncations remain
   inconclusive.
3. The teacher uses public tier degree information to prune alternate
   assignments. It does not read private targets or certificates, but this
   source-faithful shortcut remains part of the accepted boundary.
4. The generated grammar reproduces official pin degrees and stack spectra but
   samples a bounded connected multigraph family; it is not a claim to enumerate
   every graph-isomorphic official-style puzzle.
5. Tier 5's degree sequence fixes its unlabeled parallel-star topology;
   its variation comes from relation coordinates, colours, geometry, stack
   index, rotation, and scatter. Coloured/layout diversity is not unlabeled
   topology diversity; other multipiece tiers vary relation topology.
6. D4 and exact sprite-set checks prevent exact semantic or sprite copies. They
   are not perceptual-similarity guarantees.
7. Candidate, assembly, and assignment searches are finite and bounded. Default
   one-level generation tries 120 candidates; exhaustion can return `None`, and
   `generate.last_rejections` preserves terminal diagnostics.
8. `ready` records generator-contract acceptance. No generated-data training or
   learned-controller evaluation has been run.
